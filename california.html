A California resident is taxed on the resident’s entire taxable income. If Kalshi profit is in that income, California taxes it at ordinary rates. Holding a contract for more than a year does not open a lower California rate.

Who is taxed

There shall be imposed for each taxable year upon the entire taxable income of every resident of this state who is not a part-year resident

RTC § 17041(a)(1)

The quoted clause is the resident rule. Part-year residents and nonresidents are under a different subsection and are outside this project’s facts. Registered domestic partners follow California filing-status rules that can differ from the federal return. The 2025 Form 540 instructions say an RDP who files single for federal must file married/RDP jointly or separately for California, with a head-of-household exception. That is a filing-status rule, not a Kalshi rule. 2025 Form 540 instructions.

No lower capital-gain rate

California does not have a lower rate for capital gains. All capital gains are taxed as ordinary income.

FTB, Capital gains and losses, last updated 28 January 2026

FTB also says all taxpayers must report gains and losses from the sale or exchange of capital assets, and that a difference in federal and state treatment of capital gains is reported on California Schedule D (540). A difference in rate is not, by itself, the reason that schedule exists. California applies its own rate schedule to taxable income. Schedule D is for a difference in the gain or loss amount.

FTB’s summary of Public Law 119-21 section 70101:

California does not conform, under PITL, to federal individual income tax rates, and instead has its own individual income tax rates that range from 1 percent to 12.3 percent. In addition, there is an additional tax of 1 percent on the portion of a taxpayer’s taxable income that exceeds $1,000,000. Thus, the maximum personal income tax rate is 13.3 percent.

FTB summary of federal income tax changes

The 13.3 percent figure is the top marginal rate, not a flat tax on Kalshi profit. Most of a resident’s income is taxed in lower brackets.

The extra 1 percent

For each taxable year beginning on or after January 1, 2005, in addition to any other taxes imposed by this part, an additional tax shall be imposed at the rate of 1 percent on that portion of a taxpayer’s taxable income in excess of one million dollars ($1,000,000).

RTC § 17043(a)

The 2025 Form 540 booklet says the Mental Health Services Tax was renamed the Behavioral Health Services Tax for taxable years beginning on or after 1 January 2025. The computation on Form 540, line 62, is 1 percent of taxable income over $1,000,000. Credits under RTC section 17039 do not reduce this additional tax. The statute says so in section 17043(c)(1).

Conformity date

On October 1, 2025, Senate Bill 711, the Conformity Act of 2025 was enacted. The Act changed California’s specified date of conformity to the IRC from January 1, 2015, to January 1, 2025.

FTB conformity, last updated 20 January 2026

The 2025 Form 540 instructions repeat the point: they refer to the Internal Revenue Code as of 1 January 2025. The One Big Beautiful Bill Act was enacted 4 July 2025. A federal change after the specified date is not a California change unless California adopts it. FTB’s summary is the place that adoption is recorded, provision by provision. It is not safe to assume conformity from a headline.

Wagering losses, if that character is used

This matters only if the contracts are wagering. The character is open. The conformity answer is not.

This provision also limits wagering losses sustained on wagering transactions to 90 percent of the amount of losses and only to the extent of the gains during the taxable year from such transactions, for taxable years beginning after December 31, 2025. California Impact: Under the PITL, California generally conforms to IRC section 165 relating to the deduction for losses as of the specified date, January 1, 2025, under block conformity pursuant to RTC section 17201, but does not conform to the clarification or limitation of the deduction made under the Tax Cuts and Jobs Act or this federal provision.

FTB analysis of Public Law 119-21 § 70114

Read that sentence narrowly. FTB says California does not conform to the TCJA clarification or to the new 90 percent limitation. It does not, in the sentence retrieved, restate the older California rule in full. Do not invent a California loss percentage from a nonconformity sentence. The next pass should line-read RTC section 17201 and the 2025 Schedule CA instructions before stating the California wagering-loss arithmetic.
