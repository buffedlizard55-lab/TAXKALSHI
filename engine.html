The same article says a missing 1099 is likely a missed issuance threshold. That sentence describes the forms Kalshi issues. It is not a de minimis exclusion for contract profit. The platform seat dissents from reading it that way, because section 61 has no such exclusion and the form list does not cover contract profit and loss.

Round 3 — Are the contracts section 1256 contracts?

CodeDissent from automatic 60/40

Section 1256(a) gives the 60/40 split and the year-end mark only to a section 1256 contract. Section 1256(b)(2)(B), quoted on this page’s source pass from Cornell LII, excludes specified swaps “or similar agreement.” A qualified board or exchange is only one element. The mark-to-market deposit-and-withdrawal element in section 1256(g)(1) was seen in a search extract of the Office of the Law Revision Counsel page. The direct fetch did not render subsection (g). That element is flagged for a line re-read and is not quoted here as if this pass closed it. What this pass does quote from the IRS is the qualified-board definition:

(B) a domestic board of trade designated as a contract market by the Commodity Futures Trading Commission

Rev. Rul. 2007-26, quoting § 1256(g)(7)
Platform and CFTCFacts against a casual claim

The Commodity Futures Trading Commission today announced that it has issued an Order of Designation to KalshiEX LLC, granting it status as a designated contract market (DCM).

CFTC Release 8302-20, 4 November 2020

Kalshi Klear is authorized to clear swaps and currently clears Event Contracts, which are the only class of swaps accepted for clearing by Kalshi Klear in its capacity as a DCO. Event Contracts are contracts that settle based on the occurrence or non-occurrence of a specified future event and are fully collateralized at all times.

Opening of the 26 March 2026 filing. Not the same paragraph as the next quote.

Event Contracts are fully collateralized and are not traded on margin.

Same filing, risk-management section 39.5(b)(3)(vi).

DCM status supports one element of a qualified board or exchange. The same company’s clearing filing calls the contracts swaps and says they are not traded on margin. A CEA “swap” label is not automatically a section 1256(b)(2)(B) “similar agreement.” The seat refuses to treat either label as the tax answer. It does refuse to treat DCM status as enough.

California FTBInsufficient on timing; settled on rate

Even if section 1256 applied federally, FTB says California has no lower capital-gain rate. Whether California would mark an open event contract to market on 31 December was not confirmed by an FTB sentence aimed at these contracts. General conformity as of 1 January 2025 is not that sentence. Left open. FTB conformity.

CPA seatsInsufficient record

Published practitioner pages disagree with each other. None is admitted. Admitting them without a board lookup would manufacture the panel the prompt asked for.

Round result: open, with the weight of the primary record against claiming 60/40 on a return that has not tested the mark-to-market element and the swap exclusion. Not a holding that the contracts fail section 1256.

Round 4 — Are they wagering?

IRS administrativeList is not a classification

Gambling income includes but isn't limited to winnings from lotteries, raffles, sports betting, horse races, and casinos.

Topic 419

“Includes but isn’t limited to” means absence from the list proves nothing. Presence of “sports betting” makes sports-outcome contracts the closest analogy and the highest risk. It does not decide non-sports contracts.

Code, on consequences only90 percent, if it is wagering

shall be equal to 90 percent of the amount of such losses during such taxable year, and shall be allowed only to the extent of the gains from such transactions during such taxable year.

26 U.S.C. § 165(d)
IRS topic page, dissenting from the statute’s current textStale page — do not follow

The amount of losses you deduct can't be more than the amount of gambling income you reported on your return.

Topic 419, retrieved 24 September 2026

The page still describes the older cap and does not mention 90 percent. The statute and FTB’s description of the federal effective date control for tax years beginning after 31 December 2025. This is a recorded irregularity, not a reason to ignore the statute.

California FTBDoes not conform to the 90 percent cap

does not conform to the clarification or limitation of the deduction made under the Tax Cuts and Jobs Act or this federal provision.

FTB summary, Public Law 119-21 § 70114

Round result: wagering character is open. If someone nevertheless uses it for 2026, federal and California loss limits are different, and a non-itemizer does not deduct the federal losses. The engine does not adopt wagering as the default for every contract, and it does not clear sports contracts of the analogy.

Round 6 — Withholding and penalties

The prompt fixes the facts: a normal filer, not subject to withholding, not subject to penalties. The engine answers the tax under those facts. It does not design a way to avoid a penalty, and it does not compute one.
PlatformNo withholding described for contract trading

The tax-documentation article describes information returns and a P&L download. It does not describe income-tax withholding on event-contract profits. Backup withholding can apply to some reportable payments when a TIN is missing. The stated facts exclude that case.

IRS and FTBPayment rules exist; penalty math is out of scope

Topic 409 says a taxable capital gain may require estimated tax. The 2025 Form 540 booklet says an underpayment penalty may apply if withholding and estimates are short. Under the stated facts, the person pays on time by some allowed method. The amount of a penalty is not a product of this desk.

Round result: the tax answer is not a withholding answer. “Not subject to withholding” means the profit is not reduced by tax taken at the platform. It does not mean the tax is zero.

Synthesis the seats will sign

  1. Report the economic profit. A missing contract-level 1099 is not a zero.
  2. Keep interest and credits off the contract line.
  3. Do not claim section 1256 on the current record.
  4. Do not report every contract as wagering. Do not ignore the wagering analogy for sports-outcome contracts.
  5. If the profit is in California taxable income, tax it at ordinary California rates. Do not import the federal long-term rate or the federal 90 percent wagering cap.
  6. Leave the CPA seats empty until a board lookup is pasted into data/panel.json with the license URL, status, and the date it was seen. The validator rejects a filled seat that has no lookup record.

That synthesis is a research posture. A licensed preparer engaged on a real account can depart from it in writing. This desk will record that departure only after the license is verified and the writing is quoted.
