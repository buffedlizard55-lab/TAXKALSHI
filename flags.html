F27 — Topic 419 lags section 165(d)

Topic 419, retrieved 24 September 2026, still says gambling losses cannot exceed gambling income and does not mention the 90 percent limit. Section 165(d), retrieved the same day, allows a deduction equal to 90 percent of wagering losses, and only to the extent of gains. FTB dates the federal change to taxable years beginning after 31 December 2025.

How to use it: for a 2026 federal year, follow the statute if the activity is wagering. Do not follow the topic page’s silence. For a 2025 year, do not import the 90 percent rule just because the Code now contains it.

F24 — Kalshi’s $600 credit figure versus the $2,000 statute

Kalshi’s 25 August 2026 help article says incentive credits of more than $600 must be reported. Revenue Procedure 2025-32, section 2.15, says the section 6041(a) base threshold for payments made after 31 December 2025 is $2,000. This pass did not determine whether every Kalshi credit is a section 6041 payment, or whether Kalshi’s operations have caught up to the statute.

How to use it: do not treat either dollar figure as an exclusion. Report the credit. Do not “correct” Kalshi’s help page from this desk.

F40 — Secondary sites contradict Kalshi on Form 1099-B

Several web articles say Kalshi issues Form 1099-B for event-contract trades, sometimes tied to a $600 gross-proceeds threshold, and that the contracts are section 1256 contracts because the exchange is CFTC-regulated. Kalshi’s own tax article says Form 1099-B “contains transaction proceeds from broker transactions (crypto transfers).” The CFTC designation is real. It is not a tax ruling.

How to use it: those secondary claims are rejected as findings. They stay linked nowhere as authority. If a later Kalshi page changes the form description, replace F22 only after the new page is quoted.

F41 — “No 1099” can be misread

Kalshi says: “If no 1099 forms appear in your account, it is likely because you did not meet the IRS thresholds required for issuance.” The forms on that page are interest, credits, crypto-transfer proceeds, and ZeroHash digital-asset reporting. A reader can treat the sentence as a de minimis rule for trading profit. Section 61 does not say that.

Section 1256(g)(1) was not closed in the direct fetch

A search extract of the Office of the Law Revision Counsel page for section 1256 included this definition of a regulated futures contract: a contract with respect to which the amount required to be deposited and the amount which may be withdrawn depends on a system of marking to market, and which is traded on or subject to the rules of a qualified board or exchange. The direct fetch of the Cornell text of section 1256 rendered subsections (a) through part of (f) and did not render subsection (g). Revenue Ruling 2007-26 was used for the qualified-board definition in section 1256(g)(7), which the search extract of that ruling did quote. It was not used as a quote of subsection (g)(1).

How to use it: do not treat the mark-to-market element as a closed quotation until the next pass reads subsection (g) on uscode.house.gov or Cornell and pastes the sentence into the ledger. The fully-collateralized fact from Kalshi Klear’s filing is closed. The statutory element it is being compared to is not fully closed.
